## Questions 1–3 · Busbecq on Ottoman appointments

**1.** **Answer: B**
- ✅ **B:** The devshirme recruited Christian boys, converted them, and trained them as Janissaries and officials. They rose by ability, not family, which is the "sons of shepherds" Busbecq describes.
- ❌ A: Tax farming was about collecting revenue, not choosing officials by merit.
- ❌ C: The millet system governed religious communities, not appointments.
- ❌ D: The Table of Ranks was Russian (Peter the Great, 1722).
- *Tags:* 3.2 · Causation · Easy · Review: 3.2 notes, ① Bureaucratic Elites

**2.** **Answer: D**
- ✅ **D:** Busbecq served the Habsburgs, where high office usually went to nobles. Ottoman merit-based promotion stood out to him because it was so different from his own court.
- ❌ A: He was a Christian European diplomat, not a Muslim scholar.
- ❌ B: He was an ambassador and was not trained in the palace school.
- ❌ C: The passage praises the Ottoman system; it does not urge the sultan to adopt European customs.
- *Tags:* 3.2 · Source analysis (point of view) · Medium · Review: 3.2 notes, ① · *The source is not in the notes; the devshirme is.*

**3.** **Answer: C**
- ✅ **C:** Both the Ottoman system and the Qing civil service exam chose officials by merit rather than birth.
- ❌ A: Trade policy, not how officials were chosen.
- ❌ B: The queue showed loyalty to the Manchus; it was not a way to choose officials.
- ❌ D: Territorial expansion, not appointments.
- *Tags:* 3.4 · Comparison · Medium · Review: 3.4 notes, Master Comparison Table

## Questions 4–6 · Luther's *Ninety-Five Theses*

**4.** **Answer: A**
- ✅ **A:** "As soon as the money clinks into the money chest, the soul flies out of purgatory" mocks the sale of indulgences, which helped pay for St. Peter's Basilica.
- ❌ B: The passage is about the Church, not the Ottomans.
- ❌ C: Predestination was John Calvin's teaching, not the target here.
- ❌ D: The Jesuits were founded later (approved 1540).
- *Tags:* 3.3 · Source analysis · Easy · Review: 3.3 notes, Causes

**5.** **Answer: B**
- ✅ **B:** The printing press let Luther's writings and Bibles in local languages spread quickly.
- ❌ A: Westphalia (1648) ended the wars of religion; it did not spread Luther's ideas.
- ❌ C: Trent was the Catholic response to Luther.
- ❌ D: The Edict of Nantes (1598) gave limited tolerance to French Protestants, long after Luther's ideas had spread.
- *Tags:* 3.3 · Causation · Easy · Review: 3.3 notes, Causes

**6.** **Answer: C**
- ✅ **C:** Monarchs took control of religion and Church property in their lands, and the Peace of Westphalia (1648) confirmed that rulers decided their states' religion.
- ❌ A: The opposite happened: papal power declined.
- ❌ B: The split became permanent.
- ❌ D: Religion stayed central to politics, as the wars of religion show. This is the trap answer, because rulers did gain power *over* religion.
- *Tags:* 3.3 · Continuity and change · Hard · Review: 3.3 notes, Effects

## Questions 7–9 · Qianlong's edict to George III

**7.** **Answer: D**
- ✅ **D:** The Qing limited European trade to Canton and saw no need for British goods.
- ❌ A: The opposite of the edict's tone.
- ❌ B: The Qing did not send fleets to Europe.
- ❌ C: The edict rejects foreign products.
- *Tags:* 3.1 · Source analysis · Easy · Review: 3.1 notes, Qing Dynasty

**8.** **Answer: A**
- ✅ **A:** Under Qianlong the Qing reached its largest size, adding Xinjiang and sending troops into Tibet. Its power made foreign trade seem unnecessary.
- ❌ B: No such war had happened; Britain would defeat China in the Opium War (1839–1842).
- ❌ C: The Qing kept the civil service exam.
- ❌ D: Silver flowed *into* China, mainly from the Americas, not from Russia.
- *Tags:* 3.1 · Context · Medium · Review: 3.1 notes, Qing Dynasty table

**9.** **Answer: A**
- ✅ **A:** Peter the Great westernized Russia by inviting foreign experts and adopting Western technology, the opposite of Qianlong's response.
- ❌ B: The jizya concerned religious minorities, not relations with the West.
- ❌ C: A military campaign, not a response to Western ideas or trade.
- ❌ D: Kangxi was a Qing emperor with an approach similar to Qianlong's.
- *Tags:* 3.4 · Comparison · Medium · Review: 3.4 notes, Relationship with the West

## Questions 10–12 · Russian expansion table

**10.** **Answer: B**
- ✅ **B:** Kazan and Astrakhan were khanates that formed from the Golden Horde, part of the Mongol Empire.
- ❌ A: The Byzantine Empire was in Anatolia and the Balkans.
- ❌ C: The Safavids were in Persia.
- ❌ D: The Ming were in China.
- *Tags:* 3.1 · Context · Easy · Review: 3.1 notes, Russia

**11.** **Answer: C**
- ✅ **C:** Cossacks with firearms led the push east, and fur traders were drawn by valuable furs.
- ❌ A: Russia had no Pacific navy at that time.
- ❌ B: Russia had no such alliance with the Mughals.
- ❌ D: Missionaries spread Orthodox Christianity, not Islam.
- *Tags:* 3.1 · Causation · Medium · Review: 3.1 notes, Russia · *Yermak (1582) is not in the notes.*

**12.** **Answer: D**
- ✅ **D:** As Russia expanded east and the Qing expanded north and west, their borders met. The Treaty of Nerchinsk set a border between the two land-based empires.
- ❌ A: Russia remained a land-based empire.
- ❌ B: Manchuria was the Qing homeland and stayed under Qing control.
- ❌ C: The table shows Russia expanding east, including to Alaska in 1741.
- *Tags:* 3.1 · Source analysis · Hard · Review: 3.1 notes, Russia; 3.4 notes · *The Treaty of Nerchinsk (1689) is not in the notes.*

## Questions 13–16 · Bossuet on royal power

**13.** **Answer: B**
- ✅ **B:** This is the divine right of kings, which justified the absolute monarchy of Louis XIV.
- ❌ A: Divine right rejects limits on the king.
- ❌ C: The passage puts the king's throne directly under God, not under the Pope.
- ❌ D: The passage says attacking the king is sacrilege.
- *Tags:* 3.2 · Source analysis · Medium · Review: 3.2 notes, ② Religious Ideas · *Bossuet is not in the notes; divine right is.*

**14.** **Answer: C**
- ✅ **C:** A book written for the heir to the throne was meant to teach a future king how to see his power.
- ❌ A: The passage strongly supports the monarchy.
- ❌ B: The intended reader was the royal heir.
- ❌ D: The source is French and describes French kingship.
- *Tags:* 3.2 · Source analysis (purpose/audience) · Medium · Review: 3.2 notes, European Absolute Monarchs

**15.** **Answer: D**
- ✅ **D:** Under divine right, no one may attack the king. Under the Mandate of Heaven, heaven can withdraw its support from a bad ruler, which can justify rebellion.
- ❌ A: The Inca idea is similar: the ruler's power came from a god.
- ❌ B: The caliph claim also tied the ruler to religious authority.
- ❌ C: The Safavids also used religion to support the ruler.
- *Tags:* 3.4 · Comparison · Hard · Review: 3.2 notes, ② Religious Ideas; 3.2 flashcard 23

**16.** **Answer: A**
- ✅ **A:** At Versailles (from 1682), Louis XIV kept nobles at court and under watch, weakening their independent power.
- ❌ B: The Table of Ranks was created by Peter the Great.
- ❌ C: The jizya was a Mughal tax.
- ❌ D: The devshirme was Ottoman.
- *Tags:* 3.2 · Causation · Medium · Review: 3.2 notes, ③ Art & Monumental Architecture

## Questions 17–18 · Akbar and Aurangzeb

**17.** **Answer: C**
- ✅ **C:** The passage contrasts Akbar's tolerance with Aurangzeb's harsh policies, followed by provinces breaking away. This supports a change from support to resistance.
- ❌ A: Provinces broke away after Aurangzeb, so the empire became less unified.
- ❌ B: Din-i Ilahi was a court faith and was not forced on all subjects.
- ❌ D: The Mughals were Muslim rulers of a Hindu-majority population.
- *Tags:* 3.3 · Continuity and change · Medium · Review: 3.3 notes, Akbar vs. Aurangzeb

**18.** **Answer: D**
- ✅ **D:** Guru Nanak founded Sikhism around 1500. It combines belief in one God with the Hindu ideas of karma and reincarnation.
- ❌ A: The caste system is much older and is not a blended faith.
- ❌ B: The ghazi ideal is the Muslim warrior ideal.
- ❌ C: The Reformation happened in Europe.
- *Tags:* 3.3 · Comparison · Easy · Review: 3.3 notes, Sikhism

## Questions 19–20 · Rulers and subjects table

**19.** **Answer: B**
- ✅ **B:** The Mughals (Muslim rulers, Hindu majority) and the Qing (Manchu rulers, Han majority) clearly differed from most of their subjects, and the Ottomans ruled large Christian and Jewish communities.
- ❌ A: The table shows the opposite.
- ❌ C: The Mughal and Ottoman rows also show diverse populations.
- ❌ D: Only the Safavids made Shi'a Islam the state religion.
- *Tags:* 3.4 · Comparison · Medium · Review: 3.4 notes, Master Comparison Table

**20.** **Answer: A**
- ✅ **A:** The millet system let Christian and Jewish communities run their own religious and legal affairs in return for paying the jizya.
- ❌ B: The Ottomans were Sunni, not Shi'a, and they did not force conversion.
- ❌ C: The queue was a Qing policy.
- ❌ D: The Ottomans traded with Europeans, including Venice.
- *Tags:* 3.4 · Causation · Hard · Review: 3.4 notes, Religious policy toward subjects
