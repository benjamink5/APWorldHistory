## Questions 1–3 refer to the passage below.

> "In making his appointments the Sultan pays no regard to any pretensions on the score of wealth or rank. . . . Those who receive the highest offices from the Sultan are for the most part the sons of shepherds or herdsmen. . . . Among the Turks, therefore, honours, high posts, and judgeships are the rewards of great ability and good service."
>
> — Ogier Ghiselin de Busbecq, ambassador of the Habsburg ruler Ferdinand I to the court of Suleiman I, *Turkish Letters*, written 1555–1562 (excerpted)

**1.** The system of appointment Busbecq describes was made possible mainly by which Ottoman practice?
- (A) Tax farming, which sold the right to collect provincial taxes
- (B) The devshirme, which took boys from Christian families and trained them for state service
- (C) The millet system, which let religious communities run their own affairs
- (D) The Table of Ranks, which tied noble status to state service

**2.** Busbecq's point of view in the passage was most likely shaped by the fact that he
- (A) was a Muslim scholar defending the sultan's religious authority
- (B) had been trained in the sultan's palace school as a young man
- (C) was writing to persuade the sultan to adopt European customs
- (D) served a European ruler whose court gave high office largely by noble birth

**3.** The Ottoman approach described in the passage is most similar to which practice in Qing China?
- (A) Limiting European trade to the port of Canton
- (B) Requiring Han Chinese men to wear the queue
- (C) Choosing officials through examinations on the Confucian classics
- (D) Making Tibet a protectorate
