## 🅰️ Key Terms

- [ ] **1. What was the Protestant Reformation?**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>
A 16th-century movement, begun by Martin Luther in 1517, that broke from the Catholic Church and split Western Christianity
</details>

- [ ] **2. What were indulgences?**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>
Payments to the Church said to reduce punishment for sins; their sale was a main target of Luther's criticism
</details>

- [ ] **3. What was the Catholic (Counter-) Reformation?**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>
The Catholic Church's response: the Council of Trent (1545–1563) reaffirmed doctrine and fixed some abuses, and the Jesuits spread the faith
</details>

- [ ] **4. Who were the Jesuits?**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>
A Catholic order founded by Ignatius of Loyola (approved 1540), focused on education and missions in Asia and the Americas
</details>

- [ ] **5. What did the Peace of Westphalia (1648) do?**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>
Ended the Thirty Years' War, recognized state sovereignty, and let rulers decide religion in their own states
</details>

- [ ] **6. What is Sikhism?**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>
A faith founded by Guru Nanak in Punjab around 1500. It blends Hindu and Islamic ideas: one God, rejection of caste, karma and reincarnation
</details>

- [ ] **7. What was Din-i Ilahi?**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>
A blended faith that Akbar promoted at his court in 1582, drawing on several religions
</details>

- [ ] **8. What is syncretism?**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>
The blending of elements from different belief systems into a new one (e.g., Sikhism)
</details>

## 👤 People

- [ ] **9. Martin Luther**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>
German monk who posted the 95 Theses (1517); taught salvation by faith alone and the Bible as the only authority
</details>

- [ ] **10. John Calvin**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>
Reformer based in Geneva; taught predestination (God has already chosen who will be saved)
</details>

- [ ] **11. Henry VIII**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>
English king who broke with the Pope over his marriage; the Act of Supremacy (1534) made him head of the Church of England
</details>

- [ ] **12. Guru Nanak**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>
Founder of Sikhism (1469–1539) in Punjab, northern India
</details>

- [ ] **13. Akbar's religious policy**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>
Tolerance: abolished the jizya (1564), held debates among religions, promoted Din-i Ilahi
</details>

- [ ] **14. Aurangzeb's religious policy**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>
Strict Sunni orthodoxy: restored the jizya (1679), destroyed some Hindu temples, executed Sikh guru Tegh Bahadur (1675)
</details>

## 🔗 Cause & Effect

- [ ] **15. Give three causes of the Protestant Reformation.**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>
Church corruption (indulgences), the printing press spreading ideas, and rulers wanting power and Church wealth
</details>

- [ ] **16. How did the printing press help the Reformation?**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>
Luther's writings and Bibles in local languages spread quickly, so people could read scripture and judge Church teachings themselves
</details>

- [ ] **17. How did the Reformation change the power of European rulers?**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>
The Pope lost authority; monarchs gained control over religion and Church property in their lands, strengthening the state
</details>

- [ ] **18. Why did the Sunni–Shi'a divide become political?**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>
The Sunni Ottomans and the Shi'a Safavids used religion to legitimize rule and rally support, so their border wars and trade fights became religious rivalry too
</details>

- [ ] **19. What did Aurangzeb's policies lead to?**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>
Revolts by Hindus and Sikhs, the Sikhs militarizing (Khalsa, 1699), and a weakened Mughal Empire
</details>

## ⚖️ Comparison & SAQ Practice

- [ ] **20. Compare Akbar and Aurangzeb.**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>
Akbar's tolerance (no jizya, interfaith debate) won Hindu support and stability. Aurangzeb's strict policies (jizya restored) caused revolts and weakened the empire
</details>

- [ ] **21. One similarity between the Christian split and the Sunni–Shi'a split, 1450–1750?**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>
In both, rulers used religious differences to legitimize their power, which led to wars between states
</details>

- [ ] **22. Why is Sikhism an example of syncretism?**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>
It combines Islam's belief in one God and equality with Hindu ideas of karma and reincarnation
</details>

- [ ] **23. How did the Catholic Reformation differ from the Protestant Reformation?**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>
Protestants broke away from the Church; Catholic reformers kept Church authority, reaffirmed doctrine at Trent, and expanded through Jesuit missions
</details>

- [ ] **24. Explain one long-term effect of the Peace of Westphalia.**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>
It established state sovereignty: states, not the Pope, decided their own affairs. This became a basis of the modern state system
</details>
