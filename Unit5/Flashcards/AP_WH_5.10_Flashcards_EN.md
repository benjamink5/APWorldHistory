## Key Terms

- [ ] **1. What is the difference between continuity and change?**
  <details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>

  Continuity is what stayed the same over a period; change is what became different.

  </details>

- [ ] **2. What is a proto-industrial system?**
  <details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>

  Pre-factory home production, such as cottage textiles, in which women earned some extra money.

  </details>

- [ ] **3. What are collective bargaining and strikes?**
  <details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>

  Union methods: negotiating with owners for all workers, and stopping work to win concessions on wages, conditions, and hours.

  </details>

- [ ] **4. What did labor parties advocate?**
  <details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>

  Minimum wages, shorter days, paid sick and holiday leave, better conditions, and health and unemployment insurance.

  </details>

- [ ] **5. What is mass production, and how did it affect consumers?**
  <details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>

  Machines making identical goods; consumer goods became more available, affordable, and varied.

  </details>

## People

- [ ] **6. What social reforms did Bismarck create, and why?**
  <details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>

  Accident insurance, unemployment insurance, and old-age pensions — the most comprehensive worker protections — to prevent socialists from demanding stronger action.

  </details>

- [ ] **7. How did Smith and Marx view capitalism differently?**
  <details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>

  Smith: private pursuit of profit brings general prosperity. Marx: the bourgeoisie exploits the proletariat, so workers should take the means of production.

  </details>

## Dates & Events

- [ ] **8. 1848 — what did protesters demand in Paris, Berlin, and Hungary?**
  <details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>

  Paris: press freedom; Berlin: a parliament to check the monarch; Hungary: freedom from Austria.

  </details>

- [ ] **9. Early 1900s — what did women in Western industrial countries finally gain?**
  <details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>

  The right to vote.

  </details>

- [ ] **10. Over 10,000 years ago — what earlier transformation is the Industrial Revolution compared to?**
  <details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>

  The first Agricultural Revolution.

  </details>

- [ ] **11. 1848 — what text said "Capital is... a social power"?**
  <details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>

  The Communist Manifesto by Marx and Engels.

  </details>

## Cause & Effect

- [ ] **12. Why did Western Europe lead industrialization?**
  <details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>

  Abundant resources, transoceanic trade routes, financial capital, a growing population, and the Scientific Revolution (influenced by Islamic knowledge).

  </details>

- [ ] **13. How did raw-material production show both continuity and change?**
  <details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>

  Continuity: the same regions kept supplying minerals, cotton, and spices. Change: steamships, railroads, and the telegraph opened new interior sources and cut costs.

  </details>

- [ ] **14. How did the middle class show both continuity and change?**
  <details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>

  Doctors, lawyers, and shopkeepers stayed middle class (continuity); managers in factories, banks, insurance, and shipping were added (change).

  </details>

- [ ] **15. Why did women's wages become essential to families?**
  <details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>

  Factory wages were so low that every family member had to work.

  </details>

- [ ] **16. Why did democracy emerge in some countries but not others?**
  <details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>

  Where the middle class was large and economically important, democracy emerged; where it was small, dictatorships remained.

  </details>

- [ ] **17. How did workers' daily lives change from the agricultural era?**
  <details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>

  Farmers set schedules by the seasons; factory workers' lives revolved around long, low-paid shifts in crowded, polluted cities.

  </details>

- [ ] **18. How were voting rights expanded, and what was their limit?**
  <details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>

  Through legislation (Britain) or protest and revolution, to city dwellers, non-landowners, and workers — but only to men.

  </details>

## Comparison & AP Writing

- [ ] **19. How did the Industrial Revolution demonstrate both continuity and change? (Essential Question)**
  <details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>

  **Claim:** The Industrial Revolution transformed production, classes, and politics, but older patterns of resource supply, gender inequality, and national rivalry continued.<br><br>**Evidence:** Change: the factory system and mass production replaced artisans; industrialists overtook aristocrats; male suffrage widened and Bismarck created social insurance. Continuity: Latin America, Africa, and Asia still supplied raw materials; women were paid less and could not vote; Enlightenment ideas of natural rights and rivalries among nations persisted.<br><br>**Reasoning:** New technology and capital changed how wealth was made and who held it, but existing global trade roles and social hierarchies adapted to industrialization rather than disappearing.

  </details>

- [ ] **20. To what extent did industrialization change women's roles from 1750 to 1900?**
  <details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>

  **Claim:** Industrialization significantly changed women's economic role but did little to change their political and wage inequality.<br><br>**Evidence:** Women's unpaid farm labor and small proto-industrial earnings gave way to factory wages as important to families as men's; yet women were paid less, denied high-wage jobs, and did not win the vote in Western countries until the early 1900s.<br><br>**Reasoning:** Low factory wages made women's income necessary, but laws and customs that kept political power with men did not change as quickly.

  </details>
