## Set 1 · Primary source (philosophical text)

### 1. Answer: **D**
*The ideas in the passage were most directly reflected in which practice of the Song dynasty?*

- **Correct choice:** (D) Selecting government officials through examinations on Confucian texts
- **Why it is correct:** The Song expanded the civil service examinations, which tested candidates on Confucian classics such as the Analects. Officials were chosen for learning and virtue, matching the passage's ideal of ruling by virtue and propriety rather than by punishment.
- **Why the others are wrong:**
  - (A) This describes feudalism in Japan and Europe; the Song placed civil officials above military commanders.
  - (B) This describes the Mexica tribute system, not the Song.
  - (C) This describes the Inca mit'a labor system.
- **Tags:** `1.1` · Skill 1: Developments & Processes · Source analysis · Easy · Review: 1.1 §2
- 📌 **Not in the notes:** The Analects 2.3 passage itself is not quoted in the notes; the notes cover Confucianism and the exams in §2–§3.

### 2. Answer: **B**
*Which of the following best explains why Song rulers promoted Confucian ideas like those in the passage?*

- **Correct choice:** (B) To justify their rule and govern through officials trained in Confucian values
- **Why it is correct:** The CED states that the Song used traditional Confucianism and an imperial bureaucracy to maintain and justify its rule. Confucian ideas of virtuous rule and the Mandate of Heaven gave the emperor legitimacy, and exam-trained officials carried out his policies.
- **Why the others are wrong:**
  - (A) The Song did not conquer Central Asia; it lost northern China to the Jurchen and later fell to the Mongols.
  - (C) The Song lost the north to the Jurchen and was later conquered by the Mongols; Confucian ideals were aimed at its own officials and subjects.
  - (D) Confucian social ranking placed merchants at the bottom.
- **Tags:** `1.1` · Skill 4: Contextualization · Contextualization · Medium · Review: 1.1 §2 (How the Song Justified Its Rule)

### 3. Answer: **A**
*Compared with Vietnam (Đại Việt) from c. 1200 to c. 1450, Japan was different in that Japan*

- **Correct choice:** (A) was ruled in practice by shoguns and a military aristocracy, not a civilian bureaucracy
- **Why it is correct:** From 1192 the Kamakura shogunate held real power in Japan while the emperor had little authority, and regional rivalries continued for centuries. Vietnam, by contrast, adopted a Chinese-style bureaucracy with civil service exams and Confucian scholar-officials. (Korea is left out of the comparison because Goryeo was itself under military rule from 1170 to 1270.)
- **Why the others are wrong:**
  - (B) Japan embraced Buddhism, including Zen, alongside Shinto.
  - (C) Neither Japan nor Vietnam was ruled by the Song; the Mongol invasions of both also failed.
  - (D) Japan did adopt Chinese writing, Buddhism, and Confucian ideas, but so did Vietnam, so this is a similarity, not a difference.
- **Tags:** `1.1` · Skill 5: Making Connections (Comparison) · Comparison · Hard · Review: 1.1 §5 (Japan; Vietnam); Comparison Table (Japan vs. Korea vs. Vietnam)

## Set 2 · Chart (estimates)

### 4. Answer: **A**
*Which development most directly contributed to the change in population shown in the chart?*

- **Correct choice:** (A) The introduction of fast-ripening, drought-resistant Champa rice
- **Why it is correct:** Champa rice from central Vietnam ripened quickly and resisted drought, allowing two (sometimes three) harvests a year in some areas. The larger food supply supported rapid population growth under the Song.
- **Why the others are wrong:**
  - (B) The Mongol conquest came later (completed 1279) and caused great loss of life.
  - (C) Buddhism spread to China centuries earlier and did not directly increase the food supply.
  - (D) The Ming voyages (1405–1433) came long after the change shown.
- **Tags:** `1.1` · Skill 5: Making Connections (Causation) · Causation · Easy · Review: 1.1 §6
- 📌 **Not in the notes:** The Tang figures (c. 750 population, 806 iron output) are not in the notes; the notes give only the Song estimates.

### 5. Answer: **D**
*The change in iron output shown in the chart best reflects which broader development in Song China?*

- **Correct choice:** (D) Growing manufacturing and commercialization (proto-industrialization)
- **Why it is correct:** Song China had the world's greatest manufacturing capability. Iron and steel output, porcelain, and silk production expanded for growing markets, and paper money and trade spread, a change historians call a commercial revolution or proto-industrialization.
- **Why the others are wrong:**
  - (A) The Song moved further toward a money economy, including paper money.
  - (B) The chart shows output rising, which reflects growing demand.
  - (C) The Song issued and used paper money, and trade grew rather than declined.
- **Tags:** `1.1` · Skill 5: Making Connections (Continuity & Change) · Continuity & change · Medium · Review: 1.1 §6 (Proto-Industrialization)

### 8. Answer: **B**
*The movement of knowledge shown on the map was most directly made possible by*

- **Correct choice:** (B) the reach of Dar al-Islam across Afro-Eurasia, including Muslim rule in Spain
- **Why it is correct:** Dar al-Islam stretched from al-Andalus to northern India and remained one cultural region with shared Arabic learning. Scholars in Baghdad gathered Greek, Persian, Indian, and Chinese knowledge, and Muslim Spain held these Arabic works; after Christian Castile took Toledo in 1085, scholars there translated them into Latin.

  - (D) The Song tributary system linked China to East and Southeast Asian states, not to the Mediterranean.

### 9. Answer: **D**
*Which of the following was a result of the transfer shown by the westward arrows?*

- **Correct choice:** (D) Thomas Aquinas and other scholars used Aristotle to develop scholasticism

- **Why the others are wrong:**
  - (A) Christian Castile made Toledo a center for translating Arabic works after 1085.
  - (B) Champa rice spread from Vietnam to China, not to Europe.
  - (C) Córdoba was the capital of the rival Umayyad rulers of Spain, not the Abbasids.

## Set 5 · Primary source (devotional poem)

### 10. Answer: **C**
*The poem best reflects which belief associated with the Bhakti movement?*

- **Correct choice:** (C) Personal devotion to God matters more than religious rituals and holy places
- **Why it is correct:** Bhakti stressed loving devotion to a single god, open to anyone regardless of caste or gender. Kabir says God is found neither in temples nor mosques nor in rituals, but close beside the seeker.
- **Why the others are wrong:**
  - (A) The poem rejects rites and ceremonies, and Bhakti challenged caste hierarchy.
  - (B) Bhakti taught that anyone could reach God through devotion, not only priests.
  - (D) Kabir rejects both the mosque and the temple; he does not call for conversion.
- **Tags:** `1.3` · Skill 2: Sourcing & Situation · Source analysis · Easy · Review: 1.3 §3 (The Bhakti Movement)
- 📌 **Not in the notes:** The poem's text and the explanations of the Kaaba and Kailash are not in the notes; the notes describe Kabir as blending Hindu and Muslim ideas and criticizing empty ritual (1.3 §3–§4).

### 11. Answer: **A**
*The poem was most likely written in the context of*

- **Correct choice:** (A) Muslim rule in northern India and growing contact between Hindu and Muslim communities
- **Why it is correct:** After 1206 the Delhi Sultanate brought Muslim rule to northern India. Sufis and Bhakti poets lived side by side, and figures such as Kabir blended Hindu and Muslim ideas, teaching that God is one whether called Rama or Allah.
- **Why the others are wrong:**
  - (B) The Gupta Empire collapsed around 550, centuries before Kabir, and the poem rejects temple worship.
  - (C) Portuguese missionaries arrived only after 1498.
  - (D) The Khmer Empire ruled mainland Southeast Asia, not India.
- **Tags:** `1.3` · Skill 4: Contextualization · Contextualization · Medium · Review: 1.3 §4–§5

### 12. Answer: **B**
*The ideas in the poem are most similar to the ideas of which other group in the same period?*

- **Correct choice:** (B) Sufi mystics who sought a direct, personal experience of God
- **Why it is correct:** Like Bhakti poets, Sufis were mystics who sought closeness to God through devotion, poetry, and music rather than formal ritual, and both movements appealed to ordinary people and outsiders.
- **Why the others are wrong:**
  - (A) Confucian scholars stressed ritual propriety and hierarchy, which the poem rejects.
  - (C) Supporting a shadow caliph was a political strategy for legitimacy, not a devotional idea.
  - (D) Scholastics stressed reason and logic, not the inner devotion the poem describes.
- **Tags:** `1.3` · Skill 5: Making Connections (Comparison) · Comparison · Medium · Review: 1.3 Comparison Table (Bhakti vs. Sufism)

### 14. Answer: **C**

## Set 7 · Primary source (eyewitness account)

### 15. Answer: **C**
*Díaz's description provides the best evidence that the Mexica*

- **Correct choice:** (C) built large, well-planned cities with causeways and stone temples in the Valley of Mexico
- **Why it is correct:** Díaz describes many cities built in the water, a straight causeway, and great stone towers and temples. Tenochtitlan was built on an island in Lake Texcoco and linked to the shore by causeways.
- **Why the others are wrong:**
  - (A) The Mexica had hunter-gatherer origins, but by 1519 they ruled great cities.
  - (B) The Spanish were amazed precisely because these were not European buildings; there had been no earlier contact.
  - (D) The passage describes cities built in the water of the lake.
- **Tags:** `1.4` · Skill 2: Sourcing & Situation · Source analysis · Easy · Review: 1.4 §3 (Migration and Founding; Chinampas)
- 📌 **Not in the notes:** Díaz's account is not in the notes, and it comes from 1519, after this unit's period; it is used here as evidence of the city the Mexica built after c. 1325. The translation was published in five volumes (1908–1916).

### 16. Answer: **B**
*Which of the following is most important to consider when using Díaz's account as evidence about Mexica society?*

- **Correct choice:** (B) Díaz was a Spanish soldier writing decades later who compared Mexico to European tales
- **Why it is correct:** Díaz's point of view matters: he was an outsider and a member of the conquering army, he wrote decades after the events, and he described Mexica cities through European references such as the tale of Amadis. Much of what we know about the Mexica comes from Spanish sources like this.
- **Why the others are wrong:**
  - (A) The attribution says he described what he himself saw on the approach to the city.
  - (C) He completed the account around 1568, decades after 1519.
  - (D) Díaz was a soldier in the conquering army, and the passage expresses wonder, not opposition to the conquest.
- **Tags:** `1.4` · Skill 2: Sourcing & Situation · Source analysis · Medium · Review: 1.4 Big Picture (sources); §3

### 17. Answer: **A**
*Which of the following best explains how the Mexica capital could support the large population implied in the passage?*

- **Correct choice:** (A) Chinampas in the lake and tribute from conquered peoples
- **Why it is correct:** Chinampas, raised fields built from mud and plants in the shallow lake, greatly increased food production. Conquered peoples also sent tribute in food, cloth, and other goods to Tenochtitlan.
- **Why the others are wrong:**
  - (B) There was no contact with Spain before 1519; wheat and cattle came with the Columbian Exchange.
  - (C) The mit'a was the Inca labor system in the Andes, not a Mexica practice.
  - (D) Trans-Saharan trade linked West Africa and North Africa, not the Americas.
- **Tags:** `1.4` · Skill 5: Making Connections (Causation) · Causation · Medium · Review: 1.4 §3 (Chinampas; A Tribute State)

### 18. Answer: **D**
*Which of the following best describes a difference between the Mexica and Inca empires?*

- **Correct choice:** (D) The Mexica relied mostly on tribute through local rulers, while the Inca ruled more directly and took labor
- **Why it is correct:** The Mexica ran a looser tribute empire: local rulers stayed in place as long as they sent tribute. The Inca built a centralized bureaucracy with provinces and governors and took labor (the mit'a) rather than tribute in goods.
- **Why the others are wrong:**
  - (A) This reverses the two empires: the Inca built the Andean road network, and the Mexica built Tenochtitlan on a lake.
  - (B) The Mexica practiced human sacrifice on a large scale; the Inca did so far less often, for serious events.
  - (C) Both were land-based empires.
- **Tags:** `1.4` · Skill 5: Making Connections (Comparison) · Comparison · Hard · Review: 1.4 Comparison Table (Mexica vs. Inca)

## Set 10 · Primary source (legal document)

### 24. Answer: **C**
*The excerpt best shows that Magna Carta*

- **Correct choice:** (C) limited the king's power to punish free men without lawful judgment
- **Why it is correct:** Clause 39 says the king may not arrest, imprison, or ruin a free man except by the lawful judgment of his peers or the law of the land, which put the king under the law.
- **Why the others are wrong:**
  - (A) The clause protects “free men” only; most peasants were unfree serfs, and it says nothing about voting.
  - (B) Magna Carta did not make the Church England's ruler.
  - (D) King John issued the document and remained king; it limited the monarchy rather than ending it.

### 25. Answer: **B**
*Which of the following best describes the context in which Magna Carta was issued?*

- **Correct choice:** (B) In a decentralized feudal system, powerful nobles could force concessions from the king
- **Why it is correct:** In feudal Europe, kings depended on nobles who held land and owed military service. Because power was shared, the barons could force King John to accept limits on royal power in 1215.
- **Why the others are wrong:**
  - (A) The Mongols never conquered England.
  - (C) The Black Death came in 1347–1351, more than a century later.
  - (D) Exam-selected bureaucracies were a feature of Song China, not medieval England.
- **Tags:** `1.6` · Skill 4: Contextualization · Contextualization · Medium · Review: 1.6 §6–§7

### 26. Answer: **D**
*Which later development in England, c. 1265–1400, best shows a continuation of the idea that the king's power should be limited?*

- **Correct choice:** (D) The growth of Parliament, which gained power over taxes
- **Why it is correct:** Parliament first met in 1265, and Edward I's Model Parliament of 1295 brought in representatives of the towns. Over time Parliament gained power over taxes, continuing the principle that the king needed the consent of others.
- **Why the others are wrong:**
  - (A) The Norman Conquest happened in 1066, before Magna Carta, and strengthened royal control.
  - (B) Edward I expelled the Jews by royal order; this did not limit royal power.
  - (C) The sack of Constantinople (1204) happened in the Byzantine Empire, not in England.

## Set 11 · Chart (estimates)

### 27. Answer: **B**
*Which of the following best explains the change in Europe's population between c. 1000 and c. 1340?*

- **Correct choice:** (B) New farming methods such as the heavy plow and the three-field system
- **Why it is correct:** The heavy plow opened northern Europe's clay soils, the three-field system raised harvests and restored soil, and a warmer climate helped. Together they caused Europe's population to roughly double between c. 1000 and c. 1300.
- **Why the others are wrong:**
  - (A) The Black Death (1347–1351) came after this period of growth and made the population fall.
  - (C) Serfdom was still widespread in this period; it weakened only after the Black Death.
  - (D) There was no large-scale migration from Dar al-Islam that explains Europe's growth.
- **Tags:** `1.6` · Skill 5: Making Connections (Causation) · Causation · Medium · Review: 1.6 §9 (Agricultural Innovations and Population Growth)
- 📌 **Not in the notes:** The population figures in the chart are not in the notes; the notes say Europe's population roughly doubled c. 1000–1300 and that the Black Death killed about one-third. The textbook says the three-field system came into use near the end of the Middle Ages, while many accounts date its spread to the 8th–9th centuries (see the ⚠️ in 1.6 §9); either way, the notes link these farming methods to population growth.

### 28. Answer: **A**
*The change in population between c. 1340 and c. 1400 most directly led to which of the following?*

- **Correct choice:** (A) Labor shortages that weakened serfdom and contributed to peasant revolts
- **Why it is correct:** The Black Death (1347–1351) killed about one-third of Europe's people. With fewer workers, surviving peasants demanded higher wages and more freedom; when lords and kings tried to freeze wages, revolts broke out (the Jacquerie, 1358; the English Peasants' Revolt, 1381), and serfdom weakened in Western Europe.
- **Why the others are wrong:**
  - (B) Fewer workers gave peasants more bargaining power, so serfdom weakened rather than grew.
  - (C) The Holy Roman Empire began in 962.
  - (D) The Crusades began in 1095, long before this population decline.
- **Tags:** `1.6` · Skill 5: Making Connections (Continuity & Change) · Continuity & change · Hard · Review: 1.6 §10 (The Black Death and Its Effects)
