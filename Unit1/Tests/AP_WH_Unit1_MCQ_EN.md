## Set 1 · Questions 1–3 refer to the source below.
*Primary source (philosophical text)*

> “The Master said, ‘If the people be led by laws, and uniformity sought to be given them by punishments, they will try to avoid the punishment, but have no sense of shame. If they be led by virtue, and uniformity sought to be given them by the rules of propriety, they will have the sense of shame, and moreover will become good.’”
>
> — Confucius, *The Analects*, Book 2, Chapter 3 (compiled c. 5th–4th century B.C.E.), translated by James Legge (1861). *The Analects* was one of the core texts studied for the civil service examinations of the Song dynasty (960–1279).

**1.** The ideas in the passage were most directly reflected in which practice of the Song dynasty?  

- (A) Granting land to warrior nobles in exchange for military service and loyalty
- (B) Ruling conquered peoples through local leaders who paid tribute
- (C) Requiring every adult man to perform labor for the state each year
- (D) Selecting government officials through examinations on Confucian texts

**2.** Which of the following best explains why Song rulers promoted Confucian ideas like those in the passage?  

- (A) To prepare officials to govern newly conquered lands in Central Asia
- (B) To justify their rule and govern through officials trained in Confucian values
- (C) To win the loyalty of the Jurchen and Mongol armies that threatened the northern frontier
- (D) To raise merchants to the top of the social order

**3.** Compared with Vietnam (Đại Việt) from c. 1200 to c. 1450, Japan was different in that Japan  

- (A) was ruled in practice by shoguns and a military aristocracy, not a civilian bureaucracy
- (B) rejected Buddhism after contact with China
- (C) was conquered by the Song dynasty and governed directly as a province of the Chinese empire
- (D) adopted Chinese writing, Buddhism, and Confucian ideas from its contacts with China

## Set 7 · Questions 15–18 refer to the source below.
*Primary source (eyewitness account)*

> “And when we saw so many cities and villages built in the water and other great towns on dry land and that straight and level Causeway going towards Mexico, we were amazed and said that it was like the enchantments they tell of in the legend of Amadis, on account of the great towers and cues and buildings rising from the water, and all built of masonry. >
