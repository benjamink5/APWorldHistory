## Set 1 · Primary source (merchant's handbook)

### 1. Answer: **C**
*The excerpt best illustrates which feature of Silk Roads trade?*

- **Correct choice:** (C) Merchants had to deal with officials who collected duties on goods along the routes
- **Why it is correct:** Pegolotti advises merchants on handling custom-house officers, clerks, and interpreters who appraise goods, which shows that states taxed trade along the routes.
- **Why the others are wrong:**
  - (A) The excerpt is about dealing with officials, so states were present.
  - (B) Pegolotti is a private merchant advising other merchants.
  - (D) The excerpt recommends presents of goods or money and says nothing about paper money.
- **Tags:** `2.1` · Skill 2: Sourcing & Situation · Source analysis · Easy · Review: 2.1 §2–§3

### 2. Answer: **A**
*The purpose of Pegolotti's advice was most likely to*

- **Correct choice:** (A) help other merchants travel and trade profitably
- **Why it is correct:** The source is a merchant's handbook, and the advice is practical: how to get goods valued low so merchants pay less.
- **Why the others are wrong:**
  - (B) The tone is practical advice, not a complaint to rulers.
  - (C) Nothing in the excerpt concerns war or religion.
  - (D) The excerpt is about customs officers, not religion.
- **Tags:** `2.1` · Skill 2: Sourcing & Situation · Source analysis · Medium · Review: 2.1 Big Picture

### 3. Answer: **D**
*Which development most directly made the kind of long-distance overland travel described by Pegolotti safer in the 1300s?*

- **Correct choice:** (D) Mongol unification of the routes under one authority that punished bandits
- **Why it is correct:** Under the Pax Mongolica (c. 1250–1350), the Mongols unified the routes, improved roads, and punished bandits, which made overland travel safer.
- **Why the others are wrong:**
  - (A) That collapse ended the first golden age and reduced trade.
  - (B) The Ming ban came in the 1420s and concerned sea travel.
  - (C) The plague disrupted trade; it did not make travel safer.
- 📌 **Not in the notes:** The handbook's title and its c. 1340 date are not in the notes; the notes give the textbook's date of 1471. With the textbook's date, the Pax Mongolica would already be over, so the question asks about the 1300s.
- **Tags:** `2.1` · Skill 4: Contextualization · Contextualization · Medium · Review: 2.1 §2; 2.2 §3

## Set 2 · Secondary source (practice passage)

### 4. Answer: **B**
*Which of the following best explains the growth of cities such as Kashgar and Samarkand described in the passage?*

- **Correct choice:** (B) Their access to river water and their position where overland routes crossed
- **Why it is correct:** The passage calls them “river-fed oasis cities.” Travelers on dry routes depended on their water and food, and Kashgar sat where the northern and southern routes crossed.
- **Why the others are wrong:**
  - (A) Both cities are inland on the Silk Roads.
  - (C) The Ming never moved their capital to Central Asia.
  - (D) The Hanseatic League traded in the Baltic and North Seas.
- **Tags:** `2.1` · Skill 5: Making Connections (Causation) · Causation · Medium · Review: 2.1 §4

### 5. Answer: **A**
*The “Chinese credit instruments” mentioned in the passage most likely refers to*

- **Correct choice:** (A) flying cash
- **Why it is correct:** Flying cash let a merchant deposit money in one place and withdraw it in another. It was created because copper coins were too heavy to carry.
- **Why the others are wrong:**
  - (B) Tin ingots were a Southeast Asian commodity currency, not Chinese credit.
  - (C) Cowrie shells were a commodity currency used in places such as West Africa.
  - (D) The tribute system was a diplomatic relationship, not a credit instrument.
- **Tags:** `2.1` · Skill 1: Developments & Processes · Contextualization · Easy · Review: 2.1 §3

### 6. Answer: **C**
*The passage most strongly supports which claim about the Silk Roads from c. 1200 to c. 1450?*

- **Correct choice:** (C) They continued older patterns of exchange but operated on a larger scale
- **Why it is correct:** The passage says the routes “did not appear suddenly,” traces them back to Rome and the Han (continuity), and says that what changed was “scale.”
- **Why the others are wrong:**
  - (A) The passage says the routes existed under Rome and the Han.
  - (B) Sea trade came to exceed overland trade later but did not replace it, and the passage describes growth.
  - (D) The passage stresses silk and porcelain, which are luxury goods.
- **Tags:** `2.1` · Skill 5: Making Connections (Continuity & Change) · Continuity & change · Hard · Review: 2.1 §1; 2.7 §4

### 7. Answer: **D**
*The expansion of export production described in the last sentence of the passage is most similar to which development in the same period?*

- **Correct choice:** (D) Increased production of textiles in India and spices in South and Southeast Asia to meet Indian Ocean demand
- **Why it is correct:** In both cases, rising long-distance demand led producers to expand production for export.
- **Why the others are wrong:**
  - (A) That is a decline in production caused by climate.

## Set 3 · Primary source (chronicle)

### 8. Answer: **B**
*Which of the following best describes a limitation of Matthew Paris's account as evidence about the Mongol conquests?*

- **Correct choice:** (B) He wrote from England without firsthand knowledge of the Mongols

- **Why the others are wrong:**
  - (A) He wrote in 1240, during the invasions.
  - (C) He was an English monk, not a Mongol official.

*The devastation described in the excerpt most directly refers to which campaign?*

- **Correct choice:** (D) Batu's Golden Horde invasions of Russia and Eastern Europe
- **Why it is correct:** Batu invaded Russia in 1236, destroyed Kiev in 1240, and defeated European knights in 1241. This fits “the eastern parts” of Europe in 1240.
- **Why the others are wrong:**
  - (A) That happened in 1258 in the Middle East, after Paris wrote.
  - (B) That campaign was in China.
  - (C) That happened in 1389–1390, long after 1240.
- **Tags:** `2.2` · Skill 4: Contextualization · Contextualization · Easy · Review: 2.2 §4

### 10. Answer: **A**
*Which of the following was a long-term effect of Mongol rule on the region of Europe described in the excerpt?*

- **Correct choice:** (A) Russian princes accepted more centralized leadership, and Moscow later led resistance to Mongol rule
- **Why it is correct:** Mongol rule led Russian princes to improve their militaries and accept centralized leadership. Moscow built an anti-Mongol coalition and won at Kulikovo in 1380, laying the foundation of the modern Russian state.
- **Why the others are wrong:**
  - (B) The opposite happened: Mongol rule cut Russia off from Western Europe.
  - (C) Russia stayed Christian; it was the Il-khanate that converted.
