## 1. Key Terms

- [ ] **What was the "New Imperialism"?**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>The rapid, competitive expansion of industrial powers (European states, the United States, and Japan) into Africa, Asia, and the Pacific from about 1870 to 1914. It was driven by industrial needs and nationalist rivalry and justified by ideologies such as Social Darwinism and the civilizing mission.</details>

- [ ] **What is Social Darwinism?**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>The application of the idea of "survival of the fittest" to human societies, nations, and races. It claimed that strong nations would naturally conquer weak ones, so imperial conquest was presented as natural and even beneficial.</details>

- [ ] **What was scientific racism?**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>False "science," such as skull measurements, that ranked human races in a fixed hierarchy with Europeans at the top. It was used to argue that colonized peoples could not govern themselves.</details>

- [ ] **How did nationalism become a justification for empire?**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>By the late 1800s, many people believed a great power had to have colonies. Empire became a measure of national prestige and a competition with rival nations, so colonies were taken even when they cost more than they earned.</details>

- [ ] **What was the civilizing mission?**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>The belief that imperial powers had a duty to bring their "civilization" (religion, law, education, science, and customs) to peoples they saw as backward. Ending the slave trade, Western law, and Western schools were given as reasons for rule.</details>

- [ ] **What was the mission civilisatrice?**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>The French form of the civilizing mission. The Third Republic claimed its empire spread the ideals of the French Revolution and French language and culture, with the long-term aim of assimilating colonized elites into French culture.</details>

- [ ] **What is paternalism in the context of imperialism?**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>Treating colonized peoples like children who needed a guardian. Kipling called them "half-devil and half-child." Paternalism denied colonized peoples the right to govern themselves.</details>

- [ ] **What was Manifest Destiny?**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>The belief, named by John L. O'Sullivan in 1845, that Americans had a God-given destiny to spread across the North American continent. It first justified westward expansion and was later extended to overseas expansion in 1898.</details>

- [ ] **What were the "means" of empire, and how do they differ from its justifications?**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>The means were the industrial technologies that made conquest possible and cheap: steamships, the Maxim gun (1884), and quinine against malaria. Justifications were the ideas that made conquest seem right: Social Darwinism, nationalism, the civilizing mission, and religious conversion.</details>

- [ ] **What was the Anti-Imperialist League?**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>A US group formed in 1898 to oppose annexing the Philippines. Its members, including Mark Twain, argued that ruling people without their consent violated the Declaration of Independence.</details>

## 2. People

- [ ] **Charles Darwin: what did *On the Origin of Species* (1859) argue, and how does it relate to Social Darwinism?**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>It explained how plant and animal species change through natural selection. It was a work of biology, not a theory of competing nations. Others, especially Herbert Spencer, applied its ideas to societies and races to create Social Darwinism.</details>

- [ ] **Herbert Spencer**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>A British thinker who coined the phrase "survival of the fittest" (1864) and applied evolutionary ideas to human societies. His ideas became the basis of Social Darwinism.</details>

- [ ] **Arthur de Gobineau**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>A French writer whose *Essay on the Inequality of the Human Races* (1853–55) claimed that races were unequal by nature and that a "white" or "Aryan" race was superior. His work came before Darwin's, which shows racial ideology did not depend on evolution.</details>

- [ ] **Thomas Macaulay**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>A British official whose Minute on Indian Education (1835) argued that Western learning was superior and that state-funded education in India should be in English. He wanted a class of Indians who were English in taste, opinions, morals, and intellect.</details>

- [ ] **Jules Ferry**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>A two-time French prime minister whose speech to the Chamber of Deputies (28 July 1885) defended colonies for markets, for naval bases, and for national greatness, and said "superior races" had a duty to civilize "inferior races." He spoke after France's defeat in the Franco-Prussian War.</details>

- [ ] **Bernhard von Bülow**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>A German foreign secretary (later chancellor) who told the Reichstag in 1897 that Germany demanded "our own place in the sun." His phrase expresses the nationalism of a new great power that wanted colonies equal to its rivals'.</details>

- [ ] **Rudyard Kipling**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>A British writer whose poem "The White Man's Burden" (1899) urged the United States to take and govern the Philippines. It described colonial rule as a heavy, unselfish duty, a classic statement of the civilizing mission and paternalism.</details>

- [ ] **David Livingstone (1813–1873)**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>A Scottish missionary and explorer of southern and central Africa. He argued that "Christianity, commerce, and civilization" would end the East African slave trade, and his fame drew more Europeans into Africa.</details>

## 3. Dates & Events

- [ ] **1757**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>The Battle of Plassey. The British East India Company became the real ruler of Bengal, the start of British rule in India and of later arguments that Britain must "improve" India.</details>

- [ ] **1813**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>The Charter Act opens British India to Christian missionaries, a victory for Evangelicals such as Charles Grant and William Wilberforce.</details>

- [ ] **1817**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>James Mill publishes *The History of British India*, a Utilitarian work that called Indian civilization backward and said rational British law and government would bring progress.</details>

- [ ] **1858**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>France, with Spain, attacks Da Nang in Vietnam, citing the persecution of Catholic missionaries; the conquest of Indochina begins. In the same year the Treaty of Tianjin opens China's interior to missionaries.</details>

- [ ] **1897**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>Bülow demands a "place in the sun" for Germany, and Germany uses the killing of two German missionaries in Shandong as its reason to seize Jiaozhou Bay in China.</details>

- [ ] **1898**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>The United States annexes Hawaii and, after the war with Spain, takes the Philippines, Guam, and Puerto Rico. The Anti-Imperialist League forms in opposition.</details>

## 4. Cause & Effect

- [ ] **Cause → Effect: How did the Industrial Revolution contribute to imperialism?**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>Factories needed raw materials (cotton, rubber, palm oil, minerals), producers needed markets, and steamships needed coaling stations. Industrial technology (steamships, the Maxim gun, quinine) also made conquest cheap.</details>

- [ ] **Cause → Effect: How did German and Italian unification affect imperialism?**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>New great powers wanted colonies as proof of their status, and older powers such as Britain and France competed harder to keep their lead. Nationalist rivalry helped turn imperialism into a race, as in the Scramble for Africa.</details>

- [ ] **Cause → Effect: How did France's defeat in the Franco-Prussian War (1870–71) shape French imperialism?**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>Losing the war and Alsace-Lorraine hurt national pride. Leaders such as Jules Ferry argued that colonies in Africa and Indochina would restore France's greatness.</details>

- [ ] **Cause → Effect: How did attacks on missionaries lead to imperial intervention?**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>Governments used them as a reason to use force. France attacked Da Nang in 1858 after Vietnamese rulers persecuted Catholic missionaries, and Germany seized Jiaozhou Bay in 1897 after two German missionaries were killed in Shandong.</details>

- [ ] **Cause → Effect: What did King Leopold II's humanitarian claims about the Congo actually cover?**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>He presented the Congo Free State (1885) as an anti-slavery, humanitarian project, but his agents forced Congolese people to collect rubber under brutal conditions. It shows how the civilizing mission could hide economic motives (see 6.5).</details>

- [ ] **Cause → Effect: How did imperialism conflict with Enlightenment ideals, and how did Europeans explain the conflict?**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>The Enlightenment proclaimed natural rights, equality, and government by consent, but imperialism denied these to colonized peoples. Racial ideology and paternalism resolved the contradiction by claiming colonized peoples were "not ready" for rights; even John Stuart Mill said despotism was legitimate for "barbarians."</details>

## 5. Comparison & AP Writing

- [ ] **Compare: How were Social Darwinism and the civilizing mission similar and different?**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>Similar: both ranked peoples in a hierarchy with Europeans at the top and both denied colonized peoples self-government. Different: Social Darwinism saw conquest as a natural struggle in which the "unfit" lost, while the civilizing mission presented rule as a duty to improve colonized peoples so they could one day become "civilized."</details>

- [ ] **Compare: How did Germany and Japan use nationalism to justify empire?**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>Similar: both were newly unified or newly modernized powers (Germany 1871; Japan after 1868) that saw colonies as proof of great-power status. Different: Germany demanded a "place in the sun" equal to Britain and France, while Japan, after taking Taiwan in 1895, presented empire as proof of equality with the West and a way to avoid being colonized itself.</details>

- [ ] **Continuity & Change: How did justifications for empire change from 1750–1850 to 1870–1900?**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>Continuity: rule was presented throughout as a benefit to the ruled ("improvement," Christianity, ending slavery). Change: early arguments (Evangelicals, James Mill, Macaulay) assumed Indians could be reformed through education and religion, while later Social Darwinism and scientific racism treated racial differences as fixed and natural.</details>

- [ ] **AP Short Answer: Explain how Social Darwinism was used to justify imperialism.**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>
**Claim:** Social Darwinism made imperial conquest seem natural and inevitable.<br/>
**Evidence:** Herbert Spencer applied "survival of the fittest" (1864) to human societies, and thinkers such as Gobineau ranked races in a hierarchy. Cecil Rhodes called the British "the finest race in the world."<br/>
**Reasoning:** If nations and races were locked in a struggle that the "fittest" would win, then conquering weaker peoples was not a moral wrong but a law of nature, and ruling them without consent seemed justified because they were said to be unable to govern themselves.
</details>

- [ ] **AP Short Answer: Explain one way missionaries supported imperial rule and one way they complicated it.**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>
**Claim:** Missionaries both strengthened and complicated imperial rule.<br/>
**Evidence:** Supported: groups such as the London Missionary Society (1795) gave the public a moral reason for empire, and attacks on missionaries gave France (Vietnam, 1858) and Germany (Jiaozhou Bay, 1897) reasons to intervene. Complicated: mission schools trained Western-educated Africans and Asians who later led nationalist movements, and some missionaries criticized settlers' abuse of indigenous peoples.<br/>
**Reasoning:** Missionaries shared the goal of spreading European values, but their own aims were religious, not political, so their schools and criticisms sometimes produced the opposition that weakened colonial rule.
</details>

- [ ] **AP Long Essay Thesis: Evaluate the extent to which ideology, rather than economic motives, drove imperialism from 1750 to 1900.**
<details><summary>▶ Answer</summary>
**Claim:** Economic motives created by industrialization were the main driving force of imperialism, while ideologies such as Social Darwinism, nationalism, the civilizing mission, and religious conversion mainly justified expansion and won public support for it.<br/>
**Evidence:** Factories needed raw materials, markets, and coaling stations, and Jules Ferry listed markets alongside his duty to "civilize"; Leopold II's humanitarian claims hid forced rubber collection in the Congo. Yet nationalism also pushed powers such as Germany ("a place in the sun," 1897) and Japan (Taiwan, 1895) to take colonies for prestige, and missionaries sometimes arrived before traders.<br/>
**Reasoning:** Because ideology often followed and excused economic interest, it explains how empire was justified more than why it began. But nationalist rivalry shows that ideology could drive expansion even when colonies did not pay.
</details>
